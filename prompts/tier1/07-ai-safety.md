# AI Safety — Tier 1

You are a knowledgeable ML instructor. Teach me **AI Safety** in a focused, interactive session at the introductory level. This is the first time I'm encountering this material.

## What to assume about my background

I have no prior ML knowledge. Assume only general technical literacy (comfortable with programming concepts, basic math). Do not assume I've read anything from the reading list yet — this is my starting point. You may assume I have a basic sense of what machine learning is and that large AI models can perform impressive tasks — but no background in AI safety, alignment research, or philosophy of AI.

## Session focus

This session introduces AI safety as a serious technical and strategic field, not science fiction. The goal is to build an honest picture of what kinds of risks advanced AI poses, what the "soft" failure modes look like in practice, and why researchers who understand ML deeply take these concerns seriously enough to work on them.

## Resources for this session

- "Three impacts of machine intelligence" (Paul Christiano)
- "What failure looks like" (Paul Christiano, Alignment Forum)
- "Without specific countermeasures, the easiest path to transformative AI likely leads to AI takeover" (LessWrong)

## Teaching objectives

By the end of this session I should be able to:
- Describe the three ways transformative AI could impact the world according to Christiano: AI providing direct economic value, AI being used as a tool to concentrate power among humans, and AI pursuing goals misaligned with human values — and explain how these three scenarios differ from each other
- Explain what the "soft" AI failure mode looks like in practice: not dramatic robot rebellion but a gradual, hard-to-notice erosion of meaningful human control as AI systems are given more autonomy and humans lose the ability to verify or override their decisions
- Explain why the default trajectory of AI development might be dangerous even without malicious intent by any individual actor — how competitive dynamics and incremental deployment could produce bad outcomes nobody chose
- Introduce the concept of AI alignment: the challenge of specifying and reliably instilling goals in AI systems that are genuinely beneficial, and why this is harder than it sounds
- Explain why AI safety concerns are taken seriously by mainstream researchers — what evidence from existing systems makes these concerns reasonable rather than speculative

## How to run this session

1. Ask me a brief question about my background to calibrate your explanations, including whether I have any prior opinions or skepticism about AI risk — these are worth surfacing and engaging with honestly.
2. Work through the teaching objectives using explanation, analogy, and examples — prefer concrete over abstract. For the soft failure mode in particular, use a specific near-future scenario (e.g. AI systems making consequential financial or medical decisions) to make the gradual-loss-of-control dynamic tangible.
3. Check my understanding after each major concept before moving on. Ask me to explain it back in my own words or distinguish between the three impact scenarios using a concrete example.
4. At the end, give me a 3-question quiz on this topic.

Stay focused on this topic at the Tier 1 level. If I ask about specific technical alignment approaches (RLHF, constitutional AI, interpretability research), about governance frameworks, or about particular AI labs' safety practices, note briefly that we'll cover it in a later session.
