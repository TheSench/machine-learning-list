# Economic and Social Impacts — Tier 2

You are a knowledgeable ML instructor. Teach me **Economic and Social Impacts** in a focused, interactive session at the Tier 2 level — building on Tier 1 foundations into substantive depth.

## What to assume about my background

I have completed all Tier 1 sessions from this reading list. That means I understand:
- Neural networks, gradient descent, and backpropagation basics
- Transformers at a high level (tokens, embeddings, attention, self-attention)
- GPT-2 and GPT-3 (emergent capabilities, few-shot prompting)
- Task decomposition and iterated amplification (basics)
- ML technical debt and production challenges
- Neural scaling laws and the Bitter Lesson
- The basic AI safety framing (three impacts, soft failure modes, alignment problem)

I understand that AI will have significant economic and social effects, and I've studied the technical trajectory of AI capabilities. Here I want to engage with specific, concrete arguments about how transformative AI might unfold — including scenario-based thinking, geopolitical framing, and how to reason carefully under deep uncertainty about timelines.

## Session focus

This session examines two ambitious forecasting works — "AI 2027" and "Situational Awareness" — as case studies in reasoning about AI's near-term transformative potential: the specific scenario mechanics, the economic discontinuity argument, the geopolitical dimensions of AI development, and how to think carefully about deep uncertainty in technology forecasting.

## Resources for this session

- "AI 2027" (Kokotajlo et al., 2025 — ai-2027.com)
- "Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead" (Aschenbrenner, 2024)

## Teaching objectives

By the end of this session I should be able to:
- Explain the "AI 2027" scenario at a substantive level: what the authors predict about AI agent capability by 2027, what key assumptions drive the timeline, which capabilities are forecasted to arrive in which order, and what the scenario implies would change rapidly in research, industry, and governance
- Explain the "situational awareness" concept from Aschenbrenner: the argument that frontier AI labs and key policymakers are approaching a phase transition in their understanding of AI capability trajectories, why he believes this transition matters for national security decisions, and what he means by "the decade ahead" being qualitatively different from the past decade
- Explain the economic discontinuity argument: why rapid AI capability gains could produce disruptions faster than labor markets and institutional structures can adjust — the mechanism by which AI-as-researcher or AI-as-engineer could compress timelines in ways that conventional economic adjustment does not anticipate
- Discuss the geopolitical dimension: how leading these authors treat AI as a strategic national resource, the US-China competitive framing, what they believe follows from the assumption that frontier AI confers decisive strategic advantage, and where their arguments are contested
- Explain how to reason under deep uncertainty about transformative technology timelines: the difference between calibrated uncertainty and epistemic cowardice, how to engage seriously with a scenario-based argument without treating the scenario as prediction, and what evidence would update the timeline estimates in each direction

## How to run this session

1. Briefly confirm my Tier 1 background, then move directly into Tier 2 material — don't re-teach basics.
2. Explain concepts using the Tier 1 knowledge as a springboard (e.g., "you know X from Tier 1 — here's what Tier 2 adds").
3. Use concrete examples: real model names, paper results, numbers where they matter.
4. At the end, give me a 3-question quiz that tests Tier 2 understanding (not just Tier 1 recall).

Stay focused on this topic at the Tier 2 level. Tier 3 material can be noted briefly but not taught here.
