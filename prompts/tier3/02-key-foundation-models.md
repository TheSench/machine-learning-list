## Session focus

Tier 3 examines the frontier of architectural experimentation: what happens when you change the fundamental unit of computation away from tokens (Byte Latent Transformer), give a model memory that updates at test time (Titans), and aggressively curate training data quality instead of scaling data quantity (Phi-4) — plus what LLaMA 2's safety-aware training reveals about tension between capability and alignment.

## Resources for this session

- "LLaMA 2: Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models" (Touvron et al., Meta, 2023)

## Teaching objectives

By the end of this session I should be able to:
- Explain LLaMA 2's key innovations over LLaMA 1, including Ghost Attention (a technique for preserving system-prompt constraints across long conversations in RLHF-trained models), its safety fine-tuning approach, and the tradeoffs Meta made between helpfulness and harmlessness
- Explain the Phi-4 "textbooks are all you need" hypothesis: how high-quality synthetic training data generated by GPT-4 enables a 14B model to exceed much larger models on reasoning benchmarks, and what this implies about data quality vs. data quantity
- Explain Titans: the neural long-term memory module that performs gradient-based updates within a single forward pass to store and retrieve information — how it differs from attention, what it enables, and why "test-time memorization" is distinct from in-context learning
- Explain the Byte Latent Transformer: how it operates at the byte level without tokenization by using a dynamic patching strategy, what efficiency gains it achieves at inference, and why bytes-not-tokens might scale better
- Explain Qwen2.5's key architectural choices and training innovations and why a mid-sized Chinese open-weights model competes with models several times its size
- Synthesize the design space: articulate the tradeoffs across training data quality, architectural novelty, and safety tuning that these five models represent
